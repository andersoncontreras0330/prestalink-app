How to calculate loan interest

The same rate can produce different interest. It all depends on which amount it is charged on.

The formula

Interest for one period = amount × rate ÷ 100

With US$10,000 at 2% monthly, one month of interest is US$10,000 × 2 ÷ 100 = US$200. What changes from one loan to another is the amount in that formula: what was lent at the start, or what is left to pay.

Flat interest: on the amount lent

This is the most common way between people. Interest for every period is charged on the original amount, and the principal is split into equal parts.

US$10,000 at 2% monthly in 5 installments: US$2,000 of principal + US$200 of interest = US$2,200 each month. In total, US$11,000: US$1,000 of interest.

Level installment: how banks do it

The installment is also the same every month, but interest is charged on what is left. At first it is mostly interest; at the end, mostly principal.

Installment = amount × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−n)

Where i is the rate for the period (2% = 0.02) and n the number of installments. With the same loan:

On the balance, with principal payments

If US$2,000 of principal is paid every month, interest keeps going down: US$200, US$160, US$120, US$80 and US$40. That is US$600. More in paying down principal.

Interest-only, single payment and running interest

Interest-only: US$200 each month and the US$10,000 with the last one. In total US$11,000.

Single payment: everything on one date. After 150 days there are 5 periods of 30: US$11,000.

Running interest: no due date. Interest runs by day while there is debt: US$10,000 × 2% ÷ 30 = US$6.67 per day. After 47 days it is US$313.33.

This is how PrestaLink splits it. Banks sometimes quote an effective rate, which counts interest on interest and gives a slightly higher number.

Before you accept a loan

  • Ask whether the rate is per month, per 15 days or for the whole loan.
  • Ask whether interest is on the amount lent or on what is left.
  • Compare the total to pay, not just the installment.
  • Ask what happens if you pay early and what happens if you are late.
