Loan late fees and how to calculate them

A late fee is what is added for paying late. To avoid arguments, agree on it before the money changes hands.

Interest and late fees are not the same

Interest is what is charged for lending. A late fee is a separate charge, only if an installment is paid after its date.

The formula

Late fee = installment × late fee rate ÷ 100 ÷ days in the period × days charged

Days charged are the days late minus the grace days. The days in the period are 30 for a monthly rate, 15 for a 15-day rate and 7 for a weekly one.

An example

A US$2,200 installment, with a 3% monthly late fee and 3 grace days, is paid 10 days late:

  • Days charged: 10 − 3 = 7.
  • Late fee per day: US$2,200 × 3% ÷ 30 = US$2.20.
  • Late fee: US$2.20 × 7 = US$15.40.
  • To pay: US$2,200 + US$15.40 = US$2,215.40.

Grace days

They are the days after the due date when no late fee is charged yet. They cover a weekend, a holiday or a slow transfer. If it is paid within the grace days, there is no late fee.

When there is no late fee

If the loan has no agreed dates, nothing becomes overdue. That is the case with running interest and principal payments: paying later only means more interest runs.

What to put in writing

  • The late fee rate and its period, or that there is none.
  • The grace days.
  • Whether it is charged on the overdue installment or on the whole debt. On the installment is the clearest.

In many countries the law caps late fees or forbids charging late fees on late fees. Check before setting one.

How PrestaLink does it

The late fee is part of the terms, before the loan exists. After that the app calculates it by day, on the amount of the installment and once the grace days end. When you record a payment, the date you received the money is the one that counts, even if you record it another day.

The rates in these examples are only examples: PrestaLink doesn't set or recommend rates. Your country's law may cap them.
