Paying down principal lowers your interest

Every dollar that lowers the principal stops generating interest from that day on.

What a principal payment is

When you pay, the money is split: first the interest owed, then the principal, which is what you were lent. A principal payment is what you pay on top of the interest. Since interest is charged on what you owe, a lower principal means less interest next time.

A month-by-month example

US$10,000 at 2% monthly, paying US$2,000 of principal every month:

MonthInterestPrincipalPaymentLeft
1US$200US$2,000US$2,200US$8,000
2US$160US$2,000US$2,160US$6,000
3US$120US$2,000US$2,120US$4,000
4US$80US$2,000US$2,080US$2,000
5US$40US$2,000US$2,040US$0

Interest adds up to US$600. Paying only the interest each month and the principal at the end, it would be US$1,000.

Paying earlier saves more

Interest runs by day. A US$4,000 payment in the middle of the month lowers the interest for the days left: US$10,000 generate US$6.67 per day and US$6,000, US$4.

Principal payment or early installment

They are not the same. Paying an installment early is paying now what was due later. Paying principal lowers the debt. With a bank loan, ask in writing that the money goes to principal and whether it lowers the installment or the term: a shorter term usually saves more interest.

How PrestaLink does it

There is a loan type with that name, Principal payments: no fixed dates, you pay down when you can and interest is charged on what is left.

On loans with installments, if someone pays a different amount, the app covers late fees first, then interest, then principal. If they paid extra, the rest lowers the following installments.

Do you owe a bank loan? You can also record it, just for yourself, and see how each payment lowers the installment.

The rates in these examples are only examples: PrestaLink doesn't set or recommend rates. Your country's law may cap them.
